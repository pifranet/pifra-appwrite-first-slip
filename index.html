Newly Appointed? How to Get Your First PIFRA Salary Slip

Congratulations — you have your appointment letter, you have reported for duty, and your first month in government service is under way. Then payday approaches and a simple question starts to worry almost every new employee: where is my salary slip, and how will I know what I was actually paid? In Pakistan's government departments, the answer runs through PIFRA, the automated payroll system that prepares pay bills and sends salary slips to employees by email. This guide explains what happens between your joining day and that first slip landing in your inbox.

What Happens Right After You Join

Your first stop after receiving the appointment order is the office of the Drawing and Disbursing Officer — the DDO. You submit your joining report there, and the office opens your service record. Expect to hand over copies of your CNIC, your appointment letter and educational documents, along with your bank account details so your salary can be credited. The DDO office also prepares the paperwork that creates your position in the payroll system. Until this basic record exists, nothing else can move forward.

Ask the dealing clerk two questions in that first week: whether your name has been entered in the payroll system against a position code, and roughly when your first pay bill will be processed.

Why the First Salary Often Takes a Month or Two

Many newly appointed employees are surprised when the first salary does not arrive at the end of the first month. This delay is common and usually not a sign that something has gone wrong. Your record has to be created, your pay fixed according to your scale, and the first bill often has to pass through the accounts office — the AGPR for federal employees or the district accounts office for provincial staff — before payment is released. If you joined mid-month, your first payment may cover a partial month and come through with the next regular payroll run instead.

During this waiting period, keep a small file of your own: a copy of your joining report and the date you submitted your documents. If a genuine problem appears later, these details make it far easier for the office to trace your record.

Where PIFRA Registration Fits In

PIFRA does two things for you. It processes the payroll behind the scenes, and it delivers your salary slip to your email address every month. That delivery only works if your email is registered against your payroll record. New employees sometimes assume the slip will arrive automatically; in practice, registration is a separate step, and the first slip is where most people discover they were never registered.

Registration is done on a simple form. You provide your personal and service details, and the email address you give becomes the address where every future slip is sent. If you have not completed this step yet, you can register with PIFRA as a new employee by filling the form carefully, exactly as your service record shows your details. Once registered, the monthly slip normally starts arriving from the next payroll cycle onward.

The Details You Must Get Exactly Right

Most first-slip problems trace back to one mismatched detail. Your CNIC number must match your service record digit for digit — a single wrong digit can block your slip entirely. Your date of birth should be the one in your official documents, not an approximate date from an old form. Your mobile number should be one you personally control and intend to keep.

Above all, choose your email address carefully. Use a personal email account that only you can open, not an office address you may lose when you are transferred. Your salary slip contains your pay, allowances and deductions — treat it like the personal financial document it is.

What to Check on Your Very First Pay Slip

When the first slip finally arrives, resist the urge to look only at the net pay figure. Start at the top: is your name spelled the way it appears on your CNIC? A spelling error here will follow you from document to document until it is corrected, so report it immediately. Next, confirm your scale and designation — an employee entered against the wrong scale will be paid wrongly every month, not just once.

Then compare the basic pay with the amount fixed for your scale, and look through the allowances: house rent, conveyance and medical allowance are the usual ones, and they should match what your scale entitles you to. Finally, glance at the deductions. Income tax and fund deductions should be modest and explainable; if one appears that you do not recognise, ask your DDO office about it while the record is still fresh. Keep a saved copy of this first slip — banks often ask new employees for it.

If the First Slip Does Not Arrive

If your salary has been credited but no slip has reached your email, check the simple causes first: the spam folder, whether you registered with the correct email address, and whether your mailbox is full. If everything on your side is right, visit your DDO office and ask them to confirm the email address recorded against your personnel number — a typing mistake at data entry is the most common culprit of all.

If the salary itself has not been credited either, the delay is most likely in the creation of your first bill, and the DDO office is again the right place to ask. One respectful follow-up visit, with your joining report copy in hand, resolves most first-pay problems.

Your first salary slip is more than a payslip — it is the first proof that your payroll record exists and is correct.
